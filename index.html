Real life, not theory

Where the cycle really breaks, and what we do about it

Pick a stage. These are composite scenarios drawn from the kinds of issues we handle across our three offices.

The problem

A new patient books a crown. The plan looks active, but the office later learns the major-services waiting period has not been met.

What we do

We check eligibility, waiting periods, frequency limits and missing-tooth clauses before the visit, then give the office a clear estimate.

The result

The patient hears the cost before treatment, and the office is not left chasing it afterwards.

The problem

A perio scaling claim goes out without the charting and x-rays the payer requires, so it is denied.

What we do

Each claim goes through a documentation check by procedure type: narratives, radiographs, perio charting and attachments.

The result

Cleaner first-pass claims and fewer resubmissions.

The problem

The payer denies a claim as a frequency limit. The office writes it off because nobody has time to check.

What we do

We read the EOB, confirm the history, and appeal with a specific reason when the denial is wrong.

The result

Recoverable money is chased, and the denial reason is tracked to prevent repeats.

The problem

Claims sit at 60–90 days with no status. Nobody is sure whether the payer received them.

What we do

We work the aging report in order, call or check portals, and log every touch.

The result

Aging shrinks, and the office always knows where each claim stands.

The problem

An EOB shows a payment below the contracted fee, and the difference gets written off by default.

What we do

We post against the fee schedule, flag underpayments and check adjustments before they are finalised.

The result

The ledger matches reality, and short-pays are challenged.
